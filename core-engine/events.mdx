Events are everything that happened. A call changing status, a call
finishing with its transcript, a turn inside a live call - each is delivered
to your systems as an event. Events are how your systems react to
conversations without polling.

The alternative is what teams build today: cron jobs polling a call-log
API, glue code diffing CRM records, a queue you run yourself to fan out
"call ended" to three services. Events replace that with one contract -
name an endpoint, and every change is pushed to it.

## The primitives

| Primitive | What it is | Where you use it |
|---|---|---|
| In-call hooks | Turn, interruption, metric and lifecycle events inside a live call | Your `AgentRunner` - [Hooks & Events](/speech-stack/hooks-events) |
| Call status callbacks | `call.queued`, `call.ringing`, `call.answered`, `call.ended` for one call, HMAC-signed when you give a secret | A `callback_url` on `calls.create()` - [Outbound Calls](/agent-sdk/outbound-calls#status-callbacks) |
| Post-call webhook | One webhook per completed call carrying [post-call data](/api/post-call-data) | The agent's [Integration](/platform/studio-view/integration) tab |

## On the spine

```
ENTITY -> CONVERSATION -> INTERACTION -> OUTCOME ---> EVENTS
```

Events sit at the end of the spine and read from it: a call's status,
transcript and outcome reach you as events. They write nothing back to the
Context Engine - they are a record of what already happened.

## What stays hidden

The delivery machinery. How events are queued, stored until delivered and
retried is not a public surface. You see events with stable schemas and
at-least-once delivery - each call status callback carries an `id` to dedupe
on; the infrastructure behind it is Unpod's to run and swap without
breaking your consumers.
